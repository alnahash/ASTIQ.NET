Our model

An Elo rating, built and tested here

A rating system written from scratch and fitted on this season’s results across 8 leagues — 2,614 matches, 156 clubs. It uses nothing but who beat whom, by how much, and where. No bookmaker price enters it at any point.

It does not beat the market, and it is not meant to look like it does. Under walk-forward validation on 6,460 Premier League matches it had never seen, it scored 0.9692 log loss against a base rate of 1.0611 and a bookmaker line of 0.9539. That is 86% of all the signal available above knowing nothing — and still 0.0153 nats behind the price.

Behind the bookmaker

Everything else we tried added nothing. Recent form, shots, shots on target, corners, home and away splits, rest days, shot conversion, league position, points from safety, dead-rubber flags — twenty-two further features across four independent categories, and the total gain over a single Elo number was zero. The results table has one real dimension in it, and this reads it off.

Ratings are only comparable within a league. These competitions never play each other, so their scales are linked by nothing at all and ranking Bayern against Barcelona here would be meaningless. England is the exception: clubs move between its two tiers every season, so a measured offset of 272.7 points puts the Championship on the Premier League’s scale. Without it a promoted side arrives rated above half the division it just joined — Coventry read 1683 before the correction and 1426 after it.

Method

How these are found

No outcome is being predicted. A separate study of 46,596 matches found that models trained on public data land measurably behind the bookmaker’s own line, so nothing here attempts to out-forecast the market.

Sharp reference

Pinnacle’s price is stripped of its margin to get a fair line. Books quoting longer than fair are the ones out of step.

No self-reference

The book being judged is excluded from the benchmark judging it, so an outlier can never drag its own reference toward itself.

The model is not sharper

Measured out of sample it scores 1.03 log loss against the market’s 0.98. It beats a base rate, and loses to the bookmaker. Where the two disagree, the market has the better record.

Worst-case pricing

Every selection is priced under three different margin models and the least favourable result is the one shown.
